ClickHouse - Cheat-sheet

What it is

ClickHouse is a real-time, columnar OLAP engine optimized for blazing-fast analytics on massive datasets, with low cost and simple operations, ideal for events, logs, metrics, and product analytics, but not suited for transactional workloads or heavy updates.

Why it exists – Motivation

  • Traditional row databases choke on analytics at scale.
  • Big data engines like Spark are fast but complex to operate.
  • ClickHouse gives sub-second analytics on billions of rows with simple SQL, cheap hardware, and minimal ops.
  • Ideal for real-time analytics, observability, metrics, logs, ad performance, fraud detection, etc.

Core concepts you need to know

  • Columnar storage – only read columns you need; massive IO savings.
  • Sparse indexes (skip indexes) – lightweight indexes allowing fast scans without heavy trees.
  • MergeTree family – the storage engine that powers partitioning, ordering, replication, TTL, and background merges.
  • Append-only – no in-place updates; uses mutation pipelines.
  • Vectorized execution – operates on blocks for CPU efficiency.
  • Materialized views – extremely common; used to pre-aggregate data.
  • ReplicatedMergeTree – built-in HA with ZooKeeper or ClickHouse Keeper.
  • Distributed tables – logical sharding across clusters.
  • TTL – auto removal of old data.

Pros

  • Insane performance for analytical workloads (billions of rows per second).
  • Very low storage cost due to heavy compression.
  • SQL-based – easy adoption.
  • Scales horizontally and vertically.
  • Real-time ingestion (Kafka, HTTP, inserts).
  • Materialized views + AggregatingMergeTree: perfect for rollups.
  • Great ecosystem (Keeper, Kafka engine, S3 integration).
  • Simple ops compared to Hadoop/Spark/Presto/BigQuery on-prem.

Cons

  • Not designed for OLTP (no heavy updates, constraints, transactions).
  • Eventual consistency on merges; background merging can cause data lag.
  • Joins need careful modeling; not as flexible as row stores.
  • Large clusters need tuning (memory, partitions, merge throttling).
  • Mutations are expensive (updates/deletes rewrite parts).
  • Replication requires Keeper – extra moving part.
  • Sparse indexing means full scans still happen if queries aren’t well-ordered.

When to use ClickHouse – Use cases

  • Real-time analytics dashboards.
  • Product analytics (Mixpanel/Amplitude style).
  • Time-series storage (metrics, events).
  • Log analytics (ELK replacement).
  • Security analytics (SIEM, fraud detection).
  • IoT event ingestion and aggregation.
  • Financial tick data processing.
  • Ad-tech pipelines (impressions, clicks, auctions).

When NOT to use ClickHouse

  • You need transactional guarantees.
  • High-rate updates/deletes.
  • Highly relational workloads with lots of joins.
  • Small datasets where Postgres is enough.
